---
title: "If You Add an Orthogonal Branch to the Universal Wave Function, No One Would Notice"
date: 2026-03-13
math: true
---

The wave function of the entire universe $|\Psi_0\rangle$ is doing its thing, happily evolving according to the unitary transformation $e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$. You, me, Schrödinger's cat, the black hole in the center of our galaxy are described by this wave function.

Someone decides to add a branch to this function so that the new wave function becomes:

$$|\Psi\rangle = |\Psi_0\rangle + |\Psi_1\rangle$$

If $|\Psi_0\rangle$ and $|\Psi_1\rangle$ are orthogonal, i.e. $\langle\Psi_0|\Psi_1\rangle = 0$, no one would notice this addition. A supernova could explode in $|\Psi_1\rangle$ at the location where you are sitting and you wouldn't notice a thing.
